# LRC-BART: design specification, version 2 (Phase 0)

Date: 2026-09-09. Status: v2, revised after the main-session check of v1; for Yuan's approval. Inputs: REVISION-PLAN.md, revision/STATE.md, revision/00-reviews/, paper/main.tex, appendix.tex, tables_figures.tex. Nothing here has been implemented. Change from v1: the shared-leaf design (one tree ensemble with two means per leaf) is shown in Section A2 to be unable to detect a regional shift, and is replaced by a two-ensemble decomposition in the style of Bayesian causal forests (Hahn, Murray and Carvalho 2020): a pooled control surface plus a sparse source-discrepancy ensemble carrying the spike-and-slab commensurate prior.

Notation. Sources $g=1$ (RCT control), $g=2$ (RWD control), residual variances $\sigma_1^2,\sigma_2^2$. Within a leaf, $n_g$ and $\bar y_g$ are the count and mean of the tree's partial residual for source $g$, $v_g=\sigma_g^2/n_g$, $\phi(\cdot;m,V)$ is the $N(m,V)$ density. Leaf subscripts $(h,\ell)$ are dropped when the leaf is clear.

## A1. Model

The RCT treatment arm keeps its own standard BART (Gaussian or AFT), unchanged. The two control sources are modelled jointly as
$$y_i=f(x_i)+D_i\,g(x_i)+\varepsilon_i,\qquad \varepsilon_i\sim N(0,\sigma^2_{g_i}),\qquad D_i=\mathbf 1\{\text{RCT control}\},$$
so the RWD follow $f$ and the RCT controls follow $f+g$. The pooled surface $f$ is a standard BART ensemble ($H_f=50$ trees, split prior $\alpha_f(1+d)^{-\beta_f}$ with $(0.95,2)$, leaf means $N(0,\lambda_f^2)$, $\lambda_f=(\max y-\min y)/(2k\sqrt{H_f})$, $k=2$). The discrepancy surface $g$ is a small, shallow ensemble ($H_g=10$ trees, $(\alpha_g,\beta_g)=(0.5,3)$) whose leaves carry
$$\theta_{h\ell}\mid z_{h\ell}\sim z_{h\ell}\,N(0,\tau_0^2)+(1-z_{h\ell})\,N(0,\tau_1^2),\qquad z_{h\ell}\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(w),$$
$$w\sim\mathrm{Beta}(a_w,b_w),\qquad \tau_0^2\sim\text{scaled-Inv-}\chi^2(\nu_0,s_0^2),\qquad \tau_1^2=\bigl(\max y-\min y\bigr)^2/(4k^2H_g)\ \text{fixed},$$
with (A1) $\tau_0^2<\tau_1^2$. The spike ($z=1$) is the commensurate component: the RCT control surface is locally the RWD surface up to a discrepancy of scale $\tau_0$, the scale that the ESS calibration sets. The slab ($z=0$) is the robust component of Schmidli et al. (2014), placed in a leaf of $g$: a free local discrepancy on the outcome scale, sized by the standard BART range rule for an $H_g$-tree ensemble. The commensurate structure is that of Hobbs et al. (2011) with the historical surface as the center, and $g$ is the nonparametric source offset that Zhou and Ji (2021) propose in their discussion as future work. Residual variances keep the paper's Inv-Gamma priors.

Nested cases. $w=1$ (all spike) is plain commensurate BART (C-BART); $\tau_0^2\to0$ additionally gives BART-CP; $w=0$ is BART with a free nonparametric source offset, the tree analogue of BART-PP. Single arm ($n_1=0$ everywhere): $f$ is standard BART on the RWD and $g$ is a prior draw, so $f_1=f+g$ with $g$ a zero-mean discrepancy process of pointwise variance $H_g\{w\tau_0^2+(1-w)\tau_1^2\}$. With $w=1$ this has the same form as the paper's application model, $f_1(x)=f_2(x)+\sum_h e_h$, $e_h\sim N(0,\tau^2)$, the two coinciding when $H_g\tau_0^2=H\tau^2$ and the $g$ partition is taken to be the $f$ partition; the difference is only which partition carries the discrepancy.

Marginal prior and discrepancy. Integrating $z$ and $\tau_0^2$, a $g$ leaf has $p(\theta)=w\,t_{\nu_0}(\theta;0,s_0^2)+(1-w)\,N(\theta;0,\tau_1^2)$, and the local discrepancy $\Delta(x)=f_1(x)-f(x)=g(x)$ is a sum of $H_g$ such draws, one per tree.

## A2. Why the shared-leaf design cannot detect regional shifts, and the decomposition that can

Shared-leaf design (v1). One ensemble of $H$ trees, two means per leaf, $\theta_1\mid\theta_2,z\sim zN(\theta_2,\tau^2)+(1-z)N(0,\lambda^2)$. Suppose the RWD surface is shifted by $\delta$ on a region. The fit must produce $\sum_h(\theta_{1h}-\theta_{2h})=-\delta$ over the leaves covering the region; both routes below fit the data equally, so only prior cost decides. (a) All $z=1$: the cheapest allocation is $\delta/H$ per tree, cost $\delta^2/(2H\tau^2)$; with $H=50$, $\delta=2$ and $\tau^2=0.2$ (the paper's calibrated scale) that is $0.2$ nats, and $0.03$ (the scale v1 would have calibrated) gives $1.3$ nats. (b) $z=0$ in $m$ trees, each absorbing $\delta/m$ under $N(0,\lambda^2)$: cost $m\,c+\delta^2/(2m\lambda^2)$ with $c=\log\frac{w}{1-w}+\log\frac{\lambda}{\tau}$ (the prior odds and the normalizing constants), minimized at $m^*=\delta/(\lambda\sqrt{2c})$ with value $\delta\sqrt{2c}/\lambda$. Route (b) is cheaper, so an indicator can flip, only if
$$\delta>2H\tau^2\sqrt{2c}/\lambda ,$$
which at $\lambda^2=0.125$ ($H=50$, outcome range 10) is $\delta>86$ for $\tau^2=0.2$ and $\delta>17$ for $\tau^2=0.03$. No shift of clinical size reaches this. The spreading under $z=1$ also feeds $\delta^2/H$ into the $\tau^2$ update, loosening it further. Consequences: (i) $z$ never flips, the borrowing map is flat, and T2 of v1 has no empirical counterpart; (ii) the function-level commensurability variance is $H\tau^2$, not $\tau^2$, so the per-leaf ESS anchor $1/(V_2+\tau^2)$ averaged across trees misstates the function-level prior precision by a factor of order $H$. The aggregation error is separate from the spreading: summing per-tree leaf posterior variances ignores the negative posterior correlation of leaf means across trees. With $H$ identical partitions, per-tree $V_2=\lambda^2v_2/(\lambda^2+v_2)$ sums to $H\lambda^2v_2/(\lambda^2+v_2)\approx\Lambda^2$ ($\Lambda^2=H\lambda^2$), whereas the posterior variance of the sum is $\Lambda^2v_2/(\Lambda^2+v_2)\approx v_2$.

Decomposition (v2). With $f$ pooled and $g$ sparse, a regional shift lands in one or two leaves of one $g$ tree, after a single split on the region boundary that the tree prior allows at ordinary cost. Under the slab the leaf pays $c'+\delta^2/(2\tau_1^2)$ with $c'=\log\frac{w}{1-w}+\log\frac{\tau_1}{\tau_0}$; spreading under the spike across the $H_g$ trees costs $\delta^2/(2H_g\tau_0^2)$. The slab wins when
$$\frac{\delta^2}{2}\Bigl(\frac1{H_g\tau_0^2}-\frac1{\tau_1^2}\Bigr)>c' .$$
With $H_g=10$, $\tau_1^2=1.25$ (range 10), and $\tau_0^2\approx0.0012$ (Section C, target 100) this is $\delta>0.34$ before leaf noise; the leaf-level version in T2 adds the RCT standard error and gives about $0.7$ at $n_1=50$ per leaf. The design makes $H_g$ small on purpose: the spike cannot hide a shift by spreading it, because there are few trees to spread over and $\tau_0$ is small. The third route, letting $f$ absorb part of the shift, misfits both sources and pays in likelihood proportionally to $n_1+n_2$. Identifiability: $g$ is informed only by RCT controls; where the RCT has no support $g$ is a prior draw and $f$ is the RWD surface, which is the honest answer, and where both sources have support $f$ and $g$ separate because $f$ is pinned by the RWD and $g$ is shrunk by the spike. We adopt the decomposition; everything below refers to it.

## B. Posterior computation

(i) Leaf marginals. Both are exact. For an $f$ leaf the partial residuals are $y_i-f_{-h}(x_i)-D_ig(x_i)$ with source-specific variances; with $A=n_1/\sigma_1^2+n_2/\sigma_2^2$ and $B=S_1/\sigma_1^2+S_2/\sigma_2^2$, $S_g$ the residual sums, the observation-level factors $\prod_i\phi(r_i;0,\sigma_{g_i}^2)$ cancel from every tree-move ratio and the leaf factor is
$$M_f=(1+\lambda_f^2A)^{-1/2}\exp\Bigl\{\frac{\lambda_f^2B^2}{2(1+\lambda_f^2A)}\Bigr\},$$
the heteroscedastic Chipman et al. factor. For a $g$ leaf the residuals are $y_i-f(x_i)-g_{-h}(x_i)$ over RCT controls only, with statistics $(n_1,\bar y_1)$, and
$$M_g=\frac{w\,\phi(\bar y_1;0,\tau_0^2+v_1)+(1-w)\,\phi(\bar y_1;0,\tau_1^2+v_1)}{\phi(\bar y_1;0,v_1)} ,$$
a two-component Gaussian mixture in $\bar y_1$. A $g$ leaf with $n_1=0$ has $M_g=1$ and its $z$ posterior is the prior.

(ii) Tree moves. Grow, prune and change for both ensembles use the standard BART tree-prior and proposal ratios with the products of $M_f$ or $M_g$ over the affected leaves; a grow move gives $M_LM_R/M_P$. All factors are closed-form, so the tree kernels target the exact posterior with leaf parameters (and $z$) collapsed; the moment-matched Student-$t$ surrogate and Theorem 2 of the paper are dropped.

(iii) Gibbs updates. $f$ leaf: $\theta\mid\cdot\sim N\bigl(\lambda_f^2B/(1+\lambda_f^2A),\ \lambda_f^2/(1+\lambda_f^2A)\bigr)$. $g$ leaf, as a block: draw $z$ from
$$P(z=1\mid\bar y_1)=\frac{w\,\phi(\bar y_1;0,\tau_0^2+v_1)}{w\,\phi(\bar y_1;0,\tau_0^2+v_1)+(1-w)\,\phi(\bar y_1;0,\tau_1^2+v_1)},$$
then $\theta\mid z\sim N\bigl(\tau_z^2\bar y_1/(\tau_z^2+v_1),\ \tau_z^2v_1/(\tau_z^2+v_1)\bigr)$ with $\tau_z^2=\tau_0^2$ for $z=1$ and $\tau_1^2$ for $z=0$. With $K_0=\sum z_{h\ell}$ spike leaves among the $L_g$ leaves of $g$ and $S_0=\sum_{z=1}\theta_{h\ell}^2$, $S_1=\sum_{z=0}\theta_{h\ell}^2$:
$$\tau_0^2\mid\cdot\sim\text{scaled-Inv-}\chi^2\!\Bigl(\nu_0+K_0,\frac{\nu_0s_0^2+S_0}{\nu_0+K_0}\Bigr),\qquad w\mid\cdot\sim\mathrm{Beta}(a_w+K_0,\ b_w+L_g-K_0).$$
If $\tau_1^2$ is given a scaled-Inv-$\chi^2(\nu_1,s_1^2)$ prior, its update is the same form with $(L_g-K_0,S_1)$, truncated to $\tau_1^2>\tau_0^2$ to keep (A1); we recommend fixing $\tau_1^2$ by the range rule, because slab leaves are few and the slab's job is to be wide. Residual variances: $\sigma_g^2\mid\cdot\sim$ Inv-Gamma$\bigl((\nu_\sigma+N_g)/2,\ (\nu_\sigma\lambda_\sigma+\sum_{i\in g}(y_i-f(x_i)-D_ig(x_i))^2)/2\bigr)$.

(iv) AFT. On the log scale, a censored subject's $\log y_i$ is imputed each iteration from $N(f(x_i)+D_ig(x_i),\sigma_{g_i}^2)$ truncated to $(\log C_i,\infty)$ and then treated as observed, as in the paper; the treatment-arm BART is imputed the same way.

(v) One-source leaves. An $f$ leaf with only RWD or only RCT data is an ordinary BART leaf with $A,B$ from that source. A $g$ leaf with $n_1=0$ is a prior draw of $(z,\theta)$. In the single-arm case every $g$ leaf is of this kind, the $f$ moves are exactly standard BART on the RWD, and $\tau_0^2$ and $w$ remain at their priors. Minimum leaf size $n_{\min}=5$ on the leaf's own data ($f$: pooled; $g$: RCT).

Cost. One standard BART on $n_1+n_2$ observations plus a small ensemble on $n_1$; the $g$ updates are $O(L_g)$. Expect about $1.2$ times BART-PP.

## C. Prior effective sample size

The prior for the RCT control surface given the RWD is $f_1=f+g$ with $f\mid\text{RWD}$ the BART posterior on the RWD and $g$ from its prior. We define the ESS at the level of the estimand, the RCT-standardized control mean $\mu=N^{-1}\sum_{i\le N}f_1(x_i)$ over the $N$ RCT covariate profiles. For a scalar with prior variance $V_\mu$ and unit Fisher information $1/\sigma_1^2$, the ELIR of Neuenschwander et al. (2020) is $\sigma_1^2/V_\mu$. Writing $V_\mu^f=\mathrm{Var}(N^{-1}\sum_if(x_i)\mid\text{RWD})$, obtained directly from the Stage-1 posterior draws of standard BART on the RWD evaluated at the RCT profiles, and $c_g=E[N^{-2}\sum_{i,j}\mathbf 1\{x_i,x_j\text{ share a leaf}\}]$ under the $g$-tree prior ($c_g=1$ for stumps, computed once by Monte Carlo), the variance contributed by $g$ given the spike/slab states is $c_g\sum_{h}\tau^2_{z_h}$. The commensurate-component ESS, with every $g$ leaf in the spike, is
$$\mathrm{ESS}_0(s_0^2)=\sigma_1^2\,E_{\tau_0^2\sim\text{Inv-}\chi^2(\nu_0,s_0^2)}\Bigl[\frac1{V_\mu^f+c_gH_g\tau_0^2}\Bigr],$$
a one-dimensional quadrature, and the prior-averaged ESS is $\mathrm{ESS}(w,s_0^2)=\sum_{k=0}^{H_g}\binom{H_g}{k}w^k(1-w)^{H_g-k}\,\sigma_1^2E[\{V_\mu^f+c_g(k\tau_0^2+(H_g-k)\tau_1^2)\}^{-1}]$. The $H_g$ scaling is explicit: the spike scale per leaf is $\tau_0^2$, the function-level scale $H_g\tau_0^2$.

Anchor. At $\tau_0^2=0$, $\mathrm{ESS}_0=\sigma_1^2/V_\mu^f$. For a single tree with leaf posterior variances $\sigma_2^2/n_{2\ell}$ and RCT leaf proportions equal to the RWD ones, $V_\mu^f=\sum_\ell(n_{2\ell}/n_2)^2\sigma_2^2/n_{2\ell}=\sigma_2^2/n_2$, so $\mathrm{ESS}_0=n_2\sigma_1^2/\sigma_2^2$ exactly; under covariate shift it is smaller, since RWD in regions without RCT patients is worth nothing for $\mu$; for the ensemble it is the Monte Carlo value, about $208$ in the Gaussian study. The BART regularization alone contributes $\sigma_1^2/(\Lambda^2c_f)\ll1$ at this level, so no subtraction is needed, unlike the leaf-level definition. The ESS depends on the RWD through $V_\mu^f$ (leaf sizes, residual scale, overlap with the RCT profiles) and on $(w,\tau_0^2,\tau_1^2)$; the current paper's quantity depends on none of these in its no-data limit.

Calibration. $\hat s^2_{0\star}=\inf\{s_0^2\in\mathcal S:\widehat G(s_0^2)\ge q\}$ with $\widehat G(s_0^2)=B^{-1}\sum_b\mathbf 1\{\mathrm{ESS}_0^{(b)}(s_0^2)\le N_{\rm target}\}$, where $b$ indexes blocks of Stage-1 draws (each block gives one $V_\mu^{f(b)}$), $q=0.95$. Stage 1 needs only $f^{(b)}(x_i)$ at the RCT profiles and $\sigma_2^{(b)}$; before RCT outcomes exist, $\sigma_1^2$ is set to $\sigma_2^{(b)2}$ or to a preliminary RCT estimate. The target is $\mathrm{ESS}_0$, not the prior-averaged ESS, for the same reason robust MAP reports the ESS of its informative component and the robust weight separately: with per-leaf indicators the prior probability of a fully commensurate surface is $w^{H_g}$ ($0.11$ at $w=0.8$, $H_g=10$), so the averaged ESS is dominated by $w$ and cannot reach a target of $100$ whatever $s_0^2$ is. Calibrate $s_0^2$; fix $\nu_0=3$, $(a_w,b_w)=(4,1)$ two-arm, $\tau_1^2$ by the range rule, $H_g=10$. For the two-arm setting, $\tau_0^2$ is updated from the $K_0$ spike leaves (of the order of $20$ to $30$), so the calibrated prior is informative but not dominant; we report the prior $\mathrm{ESS}_0$ and the realized ESS, $E_{\rm post}[\sigma_1^2/\{V_\mu^f+c_g\sum_h\tau^2_{z_h}\}]$ with $(z,\tau_0^2)$ from the posterior. A pointwise version, $\mathrm{ESS}(x)=\sigma_1^2/\{V_f(x)+H_g\tau_0^2\}$ with $V_f(x)$ the RWD posterior variance at $x$, gives the local ESS map of Section F.

Mismatch and repair. The current definition (main.tex eq. gmap-leaf) measures curvature of a zero-centered scale mixture, $\sigma^2c_\nu/s^2$ in the no-data limit, which is regularization strength, and the $N_{\rm target}$-insensitivity of Table 1 is its symptom. The new definition is the precision of the RWD-informed prior for the estimand, anchored at the RWD sample size.

## D. Theory (Phase 3)

Single $g$ leaf, $f$ and $(\sigma_1^2,\tau_0^2,\tau_1^2,w)$ fixed, $\bar y_1\mid\theta\sim N(\theta,v_1)$, (A1) in force. Write $\hat\theta_z=\tau_z^2\bar y_1/(\tau_z^2+v_1)$: $\hat\theta_1$ is the slab estimate (close to $\bar y_1$, the RCT-only residual mean, since $\tau_1^2\gg v_1$) and $\hat\theta_0$ the spike-shrunk estimate (close to $0$, i.e. full borrowing of the pooled surface). Let $p=P(z=0\mid\bar y_1)$ be the slab probability.

T1 (bounded local influence). $E[\theta\mid\bar y_1]=p\,\hat\theta_1+(1-p)\,\hat\theta_0$ with
$$\log\frac{p}{1-p}=\log\frac{1-w}{w}+\frac12\log\frac{\tau_0^2+v_1}{\tau_1^2+v_1}+\kappa\,\bar y_1^2,\qquad \kappa=\frac12\Bigl(\frac1{\tau_0^2+v_1}-\frac1{\tau_1^2+v_1}\Bigr)>0 .$$
The RWD-induced shift relative to the RCT-driven estimate is $I(\bar y_1)=(1-p)(\hat\theta_0-\hat\theta_1)=-(1-p)\,\bar y_1\bigl(\tfrac{\tau_1^2}{\tau_1^2+v_1}-\tfrac{\tau_0^2}{\tau_0^2+v_1}\bigr)$, and $1-p\le C e^{-\kappa\bar y_1^2}$, so $\sup|I|<\infty$ and $I\to0$ at a Gaussian rate as $|\bar y_1|\to\infty$. Proof: total expectation over $z$; the odds are $M_g$'s two terms; $\kappa>0$ is (A1); $|u|e^{-\kappa u^2}$ is bounded. Under $w=1$, $I$ is linear and unbounded (C-BART, BART-CP); (A1) failing swaps the roles of the components.

T2 (borrowing map and resolution). $P(z=1\mid\bar y_1)=1-p$ is the function above of $(\bar y_1,n_1)$ through $v_1$. The half-borrowing discrepancy solves $\kappa\bar y_1^2=\log\frac{w}{1-w}+\frac12\log\frac{\tau_1^2+v_1}{\tau_0^2+v_1}$, so a leaf resolves a shift only beyond a few multiples of $\sqrt{\tau_0^2+\sigma_1^2/n_1}$ (about $0.7$ at $n_1=50$, $\sigma_1=1.5$, $\tau_0^2\approx0.001$); as $n_1\to\infty$, $1-p\to0$ for any $\bar y_1\ne0$ and $1-p\to w\sqrt{\tau_1^2/\tau_0^2}/\{w\sqrt{\tau_1^2/\tau_0^2}+1-w\}>w$ at $\bar y_1=0$. Combined with A2, the shift is absorbed by one slab leaf rather than spread. The ensemble map is $B(x)=P(\text{all }H_g\text{ leaves covering }x\text{ are spike}\mid\text{data})$, the posterior probability that $f_1$ is commensurate at $x$, computed as the fraction of draws; its natural scale is $w^{H_g}$ a priori and about $0.95^{H_g}\approx0.6$ in a compatible region a posteriori, near $0$ in a shifted region. We display $B(x)$ together with the posterior mean and interval of $g(x)$, the estimated local discrepancy.

T3 (monotone ESS, well-posed calibration). $\mathrm{ESS}_0(s_0^2)$ is continuous and strictly decreasing in $s_0^2$, with limit $\sigma_1^2/V_\mu^f$ as $s_0^2\to0$ and $0$ as $s_0^2\to\infty$; $\mathrm{ESS}(w,s_0^2)$ is in addition increasing in $w$ (binomial stochastic order) and decreasing in $\tau_1^2$. Proof: scaled-Inv-$\chi^2(\nu_0,s_0^2)$ is a scale family, so $\tau_0^2$ is stochastically increasing in $s_0^2$, and $t\mapsto\{V_\mu^f+c_gH_gt\}^{-1}$ is strictly decreasing; dominated convergence for the limits. Theorem 3(b),(c) of the paper (half-line sublevel sets, $G$ non-decreasing and right-continuous, unique infimum, Monte Carlo consistency and CLT) carry over with this closed form as the anchor; feasibility requires $N_{\rm target}<\sigma_1^2/V_\mu^f$. Theorem 1 of the paper is replaced by T1, Theorem 2 and Lemma S2.1, Definition S2.2, Assumption S2.4 are dropped, and Remark S1.2 survives as the $w=1$ contrast.

## E. Simulation design (Phase 2)

Reused mechanisms. $X_j\sim N(\mu_j,1)$, $\mu=(1,-1,0.5,-1,2,-2,2,-3,1,0)$, $X_1$ replaced with probability $0.15$ by $N(\pm3,0.5^2)$; $n_{\rm RCT}=300$ (2:1 Gaussian, 3:1 survival), $n_{\rm RWD}=300$. Sc1: $D\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(0.5)$. Sc2: $P(D=1\mid X)=\mathrm{logit}^{-1}\{(\ell-q_{0.5}(\ell))/0.5\}$, $\ell=-1.20(X_5+X_6)$. Sc3: the same with $(U_1,U_2)$ for $(X_5,X_6)$, $X_{j+4}=\rho\tilde U_j+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\epsilon_j$, $\rho\in\{-0.5,0,0.5\}$. Outcomes $Y=\alpha+f(W;\beta)+\delta Z+\varepsilon$ with the paper's nonlinear $f$, $\beta_{\rm RCT}=\beta_{\rm RWD}=(-0.50,-0.75,-0.50,-0.50,-1.35,-0.80,-0.01,-0.01,-0.01,-0.01)$, $\alpha=0.42$ (Sc1) or $-0.08$ (Sc2, Sc3), $\delta=1$, $\sigma_{\rm RCT}=1.5$, $\sigma_{\rm RWD}=1.8$; survival on the log scale with $\alpha=-0.20$ or $-0.70$, $\delta=\log1.4$, $\sigma_{\rm RCT}=1.4$, $\sigma_{\rm RWD}=1.8$, dropout Exp$(0.02)$, administrative censoring at $3-U(0,1)$.

Sc4 (partial compatibility). Covariates and assignment as Sc1; RWD outcome $Y=\alpha+f(X;\beta)+\delta_{\rm RWD}\mathbf 1\{X\notin\mathcal R\}+\varepsilon$, RCT unchanged. Primary $\mathcal R=\{X_5>2\}$ ($50\%$ of the RCT population, on the covariate with the dominant nonlinear effect, so the shift must be separated from a slope $f$ already models); secondary $\mathcal R'=\{X_7\le2\}$, a covariate with negligible effect, so a $g$ split on it is driven only by the discrepancy. $\delta_{\rm RWD}\in\{0.5,1,2\}$ (one-third to $1.33$ residual SDs), $\delta_{\rm RWD}=0$ run once in development to check Sc4 reproduces Sc1; optional slope variant with $\beta_5$ halved in $\mathcal R^c$ for the RWD. Survival Sc4: $\mathcal R$ with $\delta_{\rm RWD}\in\{0.5,1\}$ on the log scale.

Sc5 (fully shifted). $\mathcal R=\emptyset$, $\delta_{\rm RWD}\in\{1,2\}$: LRC-BART must abstain everywhere and match BART-NP; C-BART shows the unbounded bias of T1's contrast.

Comparators. The paper's set (LM-NP/CP/PP, BART-NP/CP/PP, PSCL, MAP at three targets, HierLM; AFT analogues for survival), C-BART ($w=1$, same $s_0^2$), CAHB (Jin et al. 2023, kernel-local commensurate precision; Gaussian only; implemented from the paper if no code is available), and LRC-BART at $N_{\rm target}\in\{50,75,100\}$ with a sensitivity at $H_g=5$. BART-PP is the primary benchmark.

Metrics. The paper's ATE bias, posterior SD, RMSE, coverage, power and arm-specific bias and RMSE; for Sc4 the control-surface RMSE over RCT profiles in $\mathcal R$ and in $\mathcal R^c$; map summaries $\bar B_{\mathcal R},\bar B_{\mathcal R^c}$ (mean of $B(x)$ over RCT profiles in each region; overall mean elsewhere) and the mean posterior $g(x)$ in each region; prior $\mathrm{ESS}_0$ and realized ESS.

Go/no-go (Gaussian, $N_{\rm target}=100$, development run). Sc3, all $\rho$: $|{\rm bias}|\le0.05$, coverage $\ge0.92$, RMSE $\le$ BART-PP $+0.02$. Sc1: RMSE $\le0.90\times$ BART-PP ($0.20$; BART-CP's $0.15$ is the floor) and power $\ge$ BART-PP $+0.05$. Sc4, $\delta_{\rm RWD}=2$: $|{\rm bias}|\le0.10$, control RMSE in $\mathcal R$ $\le0.90\times$ BART-PP's in $\mathcal R$, $\bar B_{\mathcal R}\ge0.5$, $\bar B_{\mathcal R^c}\le0.15$, mean $g$ within $0.25$ of $0$ in $\mathcal R$ and of $-2$ in $\mathcal R^c$; $\delta_{\rm RWD}=1$: $|{\rm bias}|\le0.10$ and overall RMSE no worse than BART-PP; $\delta_{\rm RWD}=0.5$ reported without a criterion (T2 predicts partial detection). Sc5, $\delta_{\rm RWD}=2$: $|{\rm bias}|\le0.10$, RMSE $\le1.10\times$ BART-NP, $\bar B\le0.15$. Failure triggers the failure-diagnoser. $R=200$ for development (Monte Carlo SE of a bias about $0.02$), $R=500$ final. The survival study reuses seeds, mechanisms, censoring, comparator code and result templates, adds Sc4, and reports median-ratio and RMST-ratio estimands as in Tables 2 and 3.

## F. Application (Phase 4)

EloKRd ($n=30$, treated only) with UCMM ($n=253$) as the sole control: $f$ is AFT-BART on UCMM ($H_f=10$ as before), $g$ a prior draw, $f_1=f+g$; estimands unchanged (standardized 5-year RMST ratio, median ratio). Without RCT controls nothing informs $z$, and slab draws only add prior noise of variance $(1-w)H_g\tau_1^2$ on the log scale, so we recommend $w=1$ as the reported model (the current application model with the discrepancy carried by $g$) and $w=0.9$ as a sensitivity. ## G. Implementation (Phase 1)

R package `lrcbart`, Rcpp core, from scratch. One tree class shared by both ensembles (split variable, cut, children, leaf id) with per-tree observation-to-leaf maps; a `Forest` object holding trees, a leaf-marginal callback and a leaf-draw callback, so the tree-move code is written once. Sufficient statistics: `LeafStatsF {n1, n2, sum1, sum2}` and `LeafStatsG {n1, sum1}`. Core: `f_leaf_logmarg(stats, sig1, sig2, lambda2)`, `g_leaf_logmarg(stats, sig1, tau0, tau1, w)`, `draw_f_leaf`, `draw_g_leaf` (returns $(z,\theta)$ by the collapsed block), `propose_grow`, `propose_prune`, `propose_change`, `tree_logprior_ratio`, `update_tau0(theta, z, nu0, s0)`, `update_tau1` (optional, truncated), `update_w(z, aw, bw)`, `update_sigma2`, `impute_censored(mu, sigma, logC, status)`. R interface: `lrc_bart(y, x, source, ...)`, `lrc_bart_aft(time, status, x, source, ...)`, `bart_fit()` for the treatment arm, `lrc_ess_calibrate(y_rwd, x_rwd, x_rct, N_target, grid, q)`, `lrc_borrowing_map(fit, x_new)`, `lrc_discrepancy(fit, x_new)` (posterior of $g$), `lrc_ess_realized(fit, x_rct)`, `predict.lrcbart()`, `lrc_ate(fit_trt, fit_ctrl, x_rct)`. Arguments must reach $w=1$, $w=0$, `single_arm = TRUE`, and `H_g`.

Unit tests. (1) Single $g$ leaf with $f$ fixed: sampler $P(z=1)$ and $E[\theta]$ match T1 over a grid of $(\bar y_1,n_1)$. (2) Two-leaf toy: $f$ fixed at truth, one $g$ tree with a fixed split, leaf A residual $0$, leaf B residual $-3\sigma_1$, $n_1=50$ per leaf: $P(z=1)>0.9$ in A, $<0.1$ in B. (3) $H_f=50$, $H_g=10$ on the Sc4 mechanism with $\delta_{\rm RWD}=2$, one replicate at $n_1=100$, $n_2=300$: $\bar B_{\mathcal R}\ge0.5$, $\bar B_{\mathcal R^c}\le0.15$, control-surface bias in $\mathcal R^c$ below $0.2$. (4) $w=0$, $\tau_1^2\to\infty$ limits and $w=1$, $\tau_0^2\to0$ reproduce the free-offset and BART-CP leaf factors. (5) Single-arm $w=1$ reproduces standard-BART marginals on the RWD. (6) `update_tau0` recovers a known scale. (7) AFT without censoring equals the Gaussian fit. (8) Phase 1 gate: BART-NP and BART-PP rows of Table 1 within Monte Carlo error.

## A3. Configuration after tuning (Phase 2b, appended 2026-09-09)

The development go/no-go run (02-validation/dev_gaussian) showed that the discrepancy ensemble of A1 ($H_g=10$, split prior $(0.5,3)$, range-rule slab, $w\sim\mathrm{Beta}(4,1)$) under-shoots a regional shift on Sc4 (mean $g$ of $-1.68$ in $\mathcal R^c$ and $-0.26$ in $\mathcal R$ at $\delta_{\rm RWD}=2$) and carries a bias of $0.06$ under the global shift of Sc3. Phase 2b (02-validation/tuning_phase2b, lrcbart/NOTES.md "Phase 2b tuning") varied $H_g$, the split prior, the slab scale, the leaf minimum, the $w$ prior, the number of $g$ sweeps and a warm start, and ran oracle fits with $f$ known. Two findings fix the configuration. First, the $\delta=2$ under-shoot is neither prior capacity nor mixing: it persists under every $g$ prior, under a warm start and long chains, and with $f$ known ($-0.13$ / $-1.93$ at $H_g\le3$, $-0.19$ / $-1.86$ at $H_g=10$); it is boundary-local, produced by the position of the $g$ cut (learned from about four RCT controls per $0.1$ of $X_5$) and by $f$ smearing the RWD step within $0.3$ of the boundary. Second, a moderate shift ($\delta=1$, or the $1.3$ of Sc3) is decided by a prior trade-off: the split-and-slab state pays the tree-rule prior (one cut among $p\times$numcut, about $6.9$ nats) plus the slab's Occam cost $\log\frac{w}{1-w}+\log\frac{\tau_1}{\tau_0}$, against a likelihood gain of order $n_\ell\delta^2/(2\sigma_1^2)$, so the posterior mixes the detected state with states in which $f$ absorbs part of the RCT shift. Fewer trees improve the $\delta=2$ map and Sc1 but raise $\tau_1$ through the range rule and with it the moderate-shift bias; a flat $w$ prior lowers the slab cost.

Final defaults: $H_g=5$, $(\alpha_g,\beta_g)=(0.5,3)$, $\tau_1^2$ by the range rule for $H_g$ trees, leaf minimum $5$ for $g$, one $g$ sweep per iteration, $w\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1,1)$; everything else as in A1 and C. At $R=60$ this gives Sc1 RMSE $0.148$ (BART-PP $0.186$), Sc3 bias $0.044$, Sc4 $\delta=1$ bias $-0.040$, Sc4 $\delta=2$ map $0.69$ in $\mathcal R$ and $0.05$ in $\mathcal R^c$ with $g$ of $-0.24$ / $-1.75$, and Sc5 abstention (map $0.01$); the differences from the A1 configuration are within one to two Monte Carlo SEs, and the v2 development run at $R=200$ is the test. The ESS target may be given as a fraction of the ceiling $\sigma_1^2/V_\mu^f$ (`N_target = c("frac", p)`); the ladder is absolute $50$ and $75$ where feasible and $0.9$ of the ceiling in place of $100$, and fractions $0.25$, $0.5$, $0.9$ in Sc2, where the ceiling is about $22$. The Sc4 criteria on $g$ (within $0.25$ or $0.15$ of the truth over a whole region) and on the surface in $\mathcal R$ are not reachable by tuning $g$; the manuscript reports the boundary-local leak, the map and the surface comparison both over the region and away from the boundary, and the moderate-shift bias against BART-PP's.
