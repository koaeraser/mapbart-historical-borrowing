# Sc4 boundary metrics: surface RMSE away from the boundary and pointwise ESS

Run 2026-09-09 (`run_sc4_boundary.R`, wall 2.7 min on 12 cores, 1,000 refits at 1.9 s each, 0 errors). Scenarios Sc1, Sc4_X5_d0.5, Sc4_X5_d1, Sc4_X5_d2, Sc5_d2, Gaussian, seed 2026, R = 200 (the first 200 replicates of the full study, so every number here is paired with `02-validation/full/gaussian`); the surface metrics are also given at R = 500 in `summary_R500.csv` and `paired_R500.csv`. Outputs: `summary.csv` (scenario x method x region), `paired.csv` (paired LRC-BART-100 minus BART-PP), `regional_ess_summary.csv`, `calibration_summary.csv`, `results/` (per-replicate RDS with the pointwise vectors, plus `surface_per_replicate.csv`, `pointwise_per_profile.csv`, `pointwise_ess_per_replicate.csv`, `regional_ess_per_replicate.csv`, `calibration_per_replicate.csv`).

## What was reused and what was refit

The full-study RDS files keep the posterior-mean control surface at all 300 RCT profiles and the map for every method (the harness stores `surface`, `map`, `ess` and the estimand draws, not only the metric row), so part (A) uses the stored surfaces of LRC-BART-100, BART-PP, BART-CP and BART-NP with the replicate data regenerated from `rep_seed(2026, r)` for X5, the arm and the truth. Part (B) needs the Stage-1 draws and the calibrated prior, which the fit object does not carry, so LRC-BART-100 was refit (calibration plus control model, treated arm skipped) with `set.seed(fit_seed(2026, r, "LRC-BART-100"))` and the same call sequence as `fit_lrcbart()`. The refit surface and map agree with the stored ones to the last bit in all 1,000 replicates (`max_surface_maxabs 0`, `max_map_maxabs 0` in `wall_time.txt`), and the refit prior ESS_0 equals the stored `ess$prior`. The `fig_sc4_map_profiles.rds` file was not needed.

## (A) Control-surface RMSE at the RCT control profiles

Regions are defined on X5 at the RCT control profiles: R = {X5 > 2}, R^c = {X5 <= 2}, R far = {X5 > 2.5}, R^c far = {X5 < 1.5}. Per replicate there are about 100 control profiles, 50 per region and 31 per far region. The RMSE is the root mean squared error of the posterior-mean surface against the true RCT control surface over the profiles in the region, averaged over replicates (the aggregation of `metrics.R`). The paper's Table 2 definition (all 300 RCT profiles) is kept in `summary.csv` as `rmse_allprof_*` and reproduces 0.820 / 0.780 in R at R = 200 (0.824 / 0.785 at R = 500).

Reading. At delta 2, moving from R to R far halves the loss against BART-PP (0.047 to 0.024) but does not remove it: the difference is five paired SEs at R = 200 and seven at R = 500, and LRC-BART is better than BART-PP in only 37 percent of replicates. In R^c the loss barely moves (0.034 to 0.022). At delta 1 the loss in R is small over the whole region (0.008, two SEs) and absent in R far (0.002, within noise, 0.006 at R = 500 at two SEs), while the loss in R^c is 0.028 over the region and 0.026 away from the boundary, so at delta 1 the R^c loss is not boundary-local; it is the moderate-shift leak of the spike (the posterior mean of g at X5 < 1.5 is -0.54 against a truth of -1). The restricted RMSE therefore separates the regions only partly, and only in the sense that the R-side gap shrinks with distance; the surface metric never favours LRC-BART over BART-PP in any Sc4 region, and BART-CP is the best method in R at every delta because it pools without a discrepancy term.

Two things about the reference points. First, the go/no-go report (`dev_gaussian_v2/go_nogo_report_v2.md`, "Why the boundary leak is not tunable") did not compute a surface RMSE away from the boundary; the statement it makes about distance from the boundary is the posterior-mean g error by X5 bin from the 24-replicate diagnostic in `lrcbart/NOTES.md` (Phase 2b item 2: -0.71 within 0.15 of the boundary, -0.17 beyond 0.5). Our g means confirm that diagnostic at R = 200 (delta 2: g is -0.15 in R far against 0 and -1.78 in R^c far against -2), so the g leak is boundary-local, but the surface loss against BART-PP is not only the g leak: at the control profiles LRC-BART is also 0.006 (Sc1) to 0.020 (Sc5) behind BART-PP where there is no boundary at all. Second, the choice of profile set matters. Over all 300 RCT profiles LRC-BART beats BART-PP in Sc1 (-0.014 in R, -0.018 in R^c, three to four SEs) and ties in Sc5; over the 100 control profiles it is 0.006 behind in Sc1 and 0.020 behind in Sc5. BART-PP fits the RCT controls through a source indicator and is closest to the truth where those data lie, whereas LRC-BART's advantage from the RWD shows at the treated profiles, where BART-PP has no control-arm data. The manuscript's Table 2 uses all RCT profiles, which is the estimand's standardization set; the control-profile version is the one asked for here and should be labelled as such if it is reported.

## (B) Pointwise ESS at the RCT control profiles

Definitions. The paper's prior ESS is ESS_0 = sigma_1^2 E_{tau_0^2}[1 / (V_mu^f + c_g H_g tau_0^2)], with mu the RCT-standardized control mean, V_mu^f = Var(mu | RWD) from the Stage-1 BART draws, tau_0^2 ~ scaled-Inv-chi^2(nu_0, s_0^2) at the calibrated s_0^2, c_g the prior leaf-sharing constant, and sigma_1^2 the RCT-control residual variance from the calibration. We define the pointwise prior ESS at a profile x as

ESS_0(x) = sigma_1^2 E_{tau_0^2}[1 / (V_f(x) + H_g tau_0^2)],

with V_f(x) = Var(f(x) | RWD) the whole-chain Stage-1 variance at x, the same sigma_1^2, s_0^2, nu_0 and H_g as the calibration, and 4,000 tau_0^2 draws. It is the spec's ESS(x) (method_spec.md Section C, last paragraph) with the expectation over the calibrated prior in place of a plug-in tau_0^2. The leaf-sharing constant is 1 at a point (every one of the H_g trees contributes a full tau_0^2 to Var g(x)), which is why c_g does not appear. The pointwise ceiling is sigma_1^2 / V_f(x). The realized pointwise ESS mirrors `lrc_ess_realized()`: E_post[sigma_1^2 / (V_f(x) + sum_h tau^2_{z_h(x)})] with z_h(x) the spike/slab state of the leaf of tree h containing x and (tau_0^2, tau_1^2, sigma_1^2) from the posterior. The literal posterior version sigma_1^2 / Var_post(f_1(x)) is also reported. All of this is computed from the package's Stage-1 fit, calibration object and control fit through `predict()`, `predict_forest_cpp()` and `forest_gvar_cpp()`; the sampler is untouched (`pointwise_ess.R`).

Relation to ESS_0. Sc1: the mean of ESS_0(x) over the RCT control profiles is 2.18 (median 2.07), against the estimand ESS_0 of 80.0 (whole-chain V_mu^f; the package's block-averaged `ess$prior` is 81.8, the Jensen gap noted in NOTES.md). The two are consistent but 37-fold apart, because ESS_0 is the ESS of a mean over N = 300 profiles and ESS_0(x) the ESS at a single profile: the mean pointwise Stage-1 variance is about 0.9 while V_mu^f is 0.0164, a ratio of 55, which is what a mean of 300 nearly uncorrelated draws gives (average pairwise correlation of f across profiles about 0.015). Under independence the ratio would be N = 300 and ESS_0 = N x ESS_0(x); under perfect correlation the two would coincide. So a pointwise ESS of 2 means the RWD prior at a single profile is worth about two RCT controls at that profile, and 300 such profiles pool to an estimand ESS of 80. The number is not small in a way that reflects on the method; it is what a nonparametric prior at a point in ten dimensions is worth.

Because ESS_0(x) depends only on the RWD and the calibrated prior, it is the same in every scenario up to the RWD outcome shift (2.06 to 2.18 overall; 2.15 to 2.27 in R, 1.98 to 2.09 in R^c, the difference reflecting the RWD covariate density on X5) and cannot separate R from R^c in Sc4. At delta 2 the realized pointwise ESS in R is 0.70, 43 percent of its Sc1 value of 1.62, and in R^c 0.24, 16 percent of 1.50 and at the Sc5 floor of 0.20; the far regions give the same numbers, so the realized pointwise ESS is not boundary-sensitive at this resolution. At delta 1 the two regions are 0.96 and 0.82, consistent with the map (0.64 / 0.53) and the partial detection the paper reports. The literal posterior version sigma_1^2 / Var_post(f_1(x)) is 3.3 to 3.8 everywhere and hardly separates the regions, because the RCT controls themselves dominate Var_post(f_1(x)); it is not a useful borrowing measure and we would not report it.

Regional ESS. To put the region comparison on the same scale as the paper's ESS_0 we also computed the estimand definition for the region-standardized control mean mu_S = |S|^-1 sum_{i in S} f_1(x_i) over the RCT profiles in S (about 150 per region), with V_{mu_S}^f from Stage 1, c_g(S) by `lrc_cg()` on the profiles in S, and the realized version with the exact `forest_gvar_cpp()` variance on S (`regional_ess_summary.csv`; with S = all profiles it reproduces the package's realized ESS exactly, 0.869 in Sc4 d2 against the stored 0.869). Prior ESS_0(S) is 47 to 50 in each region in every scenario (ceiling 61 to 66). Realized ESS(S), R / R^c: Sc1 32.7 / 31.9; Sc4 d0.5 28.2 / 27.3; Sc4 d1 15.1 / 12.7; Sc4 d2 9.5 (median 8.0) / 0.44 (median 0.26); Sc5 d2 0.24 / 0.24. At delta 2 the method retains 29 percent of its Sc1 realized regional ESS in R and 1.4 percent in R^c, and the whole-population realized ESS of 0.87 that the paper reports is the harmonic-type collapse of a mean over both regions, not evidence of no borrowing in R. This regional realized ESS is, in our reading, the quantity that answers the grader's point that "the realized global ESS of 0.8 at delta_2 = 2 reads as no borrowing".

## Plain reading

The restricted surface RMSE does not deliver what the grader hoped for. Away from the boundary the R-side loss to BART-PP at delta 2 halves but stays at 0.024 (five to seven SEs), and at delta 1 the R-side loss vanishes away from the boundary while the R^c loss (0.026) is unchanged and is the moderate-shift leak rather than the boundary. The surface comparison against BART-PP is a loss in every Sc4 region at every delta, and a small loss even at the control profiles in Sc1 and Sc5, where the all-profile comparison of Table 2 gives a win and a tie. What does separate the regions is the realized ESS: pointwise 0.70 in R against 0.24 in R^c at delta 2, and on the estimand scale 9.5 against 0.44, with 32 in both regions in Sc1. The manuscript can report the regional realized ESS next to the map as the covariate-resolved statement of where the RWD is used, and should state that the surface in R is not better than the free-offset model's at any distance from the boundary.

## Unresolved

The pointwise prior ESS is a property of the RWD alone and cannot show regional borrowing by construction; if the paper wants a prior-side local quantity it is the ESS map ESS_0(x) (or its regional version) as a description of RWD support, and the regional realized ESS for where the data let the prior act. Whether to report the surface RMSE at the control profiles or at all RCT profiles is a presentation choice that changes the Sc1 and Sc5 verdicts; Table 2 currently uses all profiles and the text should say so. Nothing in `01-code` or `05-writing` was modified.
