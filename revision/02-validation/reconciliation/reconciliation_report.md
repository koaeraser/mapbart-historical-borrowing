# Reconciliation of the published tables with Yunxuan's repository code

Date 2026-09-09. Line numbers refer to files under `yunxuan-repo/`; scripts, outputs and log extracts are in `runs/`. The repo's `res/` and data folders are empty and its C++ samplers (`cwbart.cpp`, `cabart.cpp`, `cmbart`, `bartModelMatrix.R`) are absent, so BART scripts cannot rerun verbatim; we reran the data generators and Stan scripts verbatim under the `run_all.R` overrides and used `BART::abart` with the repo's settings in place of `cabart`.

## Answers

**1. Power.** `decision <- mean(post_samples > eff_star)` with `eff_star = 0.5` (Gaussian) or 1 (survival ratio), `tp <- decision > 0.95`, averaged in `plot.R` 686. At delta = 1 with posterior SD 0.20 this gives 0.77 for BART-NP in Sc1, which is what reference_check found under the label "one-sided 95%, effect 0.5"; the two are the same rule. A null-calibrated threshold (`tp_calibrated`) exists in the code but no null run was made, so the published column is the uncalibrated 0.95 rule. The paper's stated definition is wrong, not the number.

**2. Two-arm Gaussian DGP.** Every stated constant matches the code and `dgp.R`. Two immaterial differences: the repo takes the first 300 D = 1 subjects of a 600-subject RCT half and the first 300 D = 0 of the RWD half (266-272, 336-340), so both sizes fall below 300 in about half the replicates (mean control arm 73 versus our 77); and the script's frozen RWD pool is overridden by `run_all.R` to fresh RWD per replicate (734), as we do. Truths are standardized to the replicate's RCT profiles in both.

**3. Survival DGP and median ratio.** The DGP matches the text. The estimand is the mixture-lognormal population median per draw, root-found with a floor at min(event time)/10 (`BARTv1.R` 340-357), each arm with its own sigma draws and the truth using sigma_RCT for both arms, all as in `estimands.R`; the one difference is the posterior median as point summary. Paired runs on the same replicates (R = 50, `runs/q3b_paired.R`): repo DGP, median-bias 0.23, mean-bias 0.28, SD 0.42; our DGP, 0.24, 0.29, 0.42; the repo's `survreg` residual-scale seed moves the bias by under 0.01, and our `estimands.R` on the repo draws returns the repo's mean-bias exactly. The published BART-NP row (Table 2, Sc1) is bias 0.10, SD 0.46, RMSE 0.44; the 0.01/0.16 quoted in RESULTS_SUMMARY appears nowhere in Table 2. Our 0.26 at R = 500 is therefore 0.05 from mean versus median plus about 0.13 that neither DGP nor `BART::abart` reproduces; that residual can only come from the missing `cabart.cpp` sampler or a run that differed from the shipped scripts, and we cannot close it.

**4. Single-arm.** The published study simulated 200 treated subjects (30 or 200 for survival), all with Z = 1, from pools of 1000 with P(D = 1) = 0.4; selection slope beta_D = -0.55 at temperature 1.0 against -1.20 at 0.5 in the two-arm study (an effective logit slope 4.4 times weaker); Gaussian intercepts 0.4 and -0.1, RWD sigma 1.5 not 1.8; survival intercepts 0.20/0.10 against the stated -0.20/-0.70, sigma 1.25 in both sources against 1.4/1.8, dropout Exp(0.09), entry U(0, 0.5), administrative censoring at 5.5 - entry, RMST horizon 5 against the caption's tau = 3. "LM-CP" is `LMv2.R`, a power-prior model at RWD weight 1 with covariates centred at the trial means. This explains LM-CP 0.28 versus our 1.68 in full: `LMv2.R` run verbatim on its own Sc2 data gives bias 0.295 (MC SE 0.033), SD 0.274, RMSE 0.441, coverage 0.72, power 0.88, control bias -0.30 at R = 100 (published 0.28, 0.27, 0.41, 0.79, 0.88, -0.29), and our LM-CP on the same 20 replicates gives 0.387 against the repo's 0.376. Our BART-CP on the v3 data gives 0.13 in Sc2 (published 0.05, R = 20) and 0.00 in Sc1.

**5. Hierarchical hyperpriors.** Both folders use `tau2 ~ inv_gamma(3/2, 3 * prior / 2)`. Two-arm: prior 0.25, IG(1.5, 0.375). Single-arm: `prior_vals <- c(0.05, 0.5)`; at 0.05 the repo reproduces the published Sc1 row (SD 0.583, control RMSE 0.60 against 0.58, 0.59; Sc2 bias 0.43, SD 0.61 against 0.35, 0.59 at R = 20), at 0.5 (the stated prior) SD is 1.58 with zero power. The repo also centres X at the trial means (single-arm `HierLM.R` 316-318), so the standardized control mean depends on alpha_2 alone and the coefficient discrepancies cancel; our harness neither centres nor uses 0.05, hence its SD 8.2.

**6. Other differences.** Linear-model priors are range-budget scales (`LMv2.R` 230-235), not N(0, 5^2) and N(0, 2^2). Stan runs one chain of 1600 with 100 warm-up; BART runs 1500 draws after 100 burn-in (`BARTv2.R` 66-67); "all Bayesian methods fitted by HMC" is false for BART, MAP-BART and PSCL. The MAP comparator uses tau^2 ~ IG(3/2, 3 s2/2) with calibrated s2 (`MAP.R` 224-225), not Half-Normal(0, 1). ESS calibration fits one BART to one replicate's RWD and uses B = 100 posterior draws (`ess_calibration.R` 92, 121-160), not M = 100 replicates, on a default grid 0.10 to 0.50 by 0.05. Near-complete pooling is s2 = 1e-4 (`mBART.R` 82); MAP-BART uses source-specific residual variances (311-321); the single-arm MAP-BART sweeps 5, 10 and 50 control trees (`run_all.R` 138) where the paper states H = 50.

## Recommendation

Tables 1 and 2 (two-arm): (a) report our harness, which reproduces the published Gaussian rows to Monte Carlo error, and restate the power and RMSE definitions to what was computed (or recompute power under the stated one); the survival BART-NP row must be replaced by ours since the published value is not reproducible from the repo. Tables 5 to 7 (single-arm): the published rows are not "the mechanisms of Sections 3.4 and 3.5"; option (b) would require restating six DGP constants, the censoring scheme, the RMST horizon and the hyperprior, and would still leave the two-arm and single-arm studies incomparable. We recommend (a): report our harness under the stated design, replace the hierarchical rows with the centred, s2-calibrated model or drop them, and state that single-arm borrowing under Sc2 is far harsher than the two-arm study.
